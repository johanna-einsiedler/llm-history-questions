You are a careful data annotator.

Your task is to determine whether a user message is a history-related fact-seeking query.

A message qualifies as HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING only if the user's primary intent is to obtain factual information about real past events, historical people, historical societies, or historical developments.

Be conservative. When in doubt, label as NOT_HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING.

Definition of HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING

Label as HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING if the user is clearly asking for factual information about the past, such as:

- causes or outcomes of historical events

- descriptions of past societies or time periods

- actions of historical figures

- timelines of real historical developments

- historically grounded explanations of past conflicts, treaties, cultures, or practices

The request must be primarily informational and historical.

Do NOT label as HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING if the request is primarily:

- literary analysis (e.g., meaning of books, themes, narrators)

- opinion or argument generation (e.g., “things to be proud of…”)

- modern political persuasion or debate

- religious or scripture lookup without historical framing

- scientific or technical explanation without historical focus

- media retrieval (find a clip, image, video)

- creative writing or roleplay

- formatting-heavy bulk tasks not focused on historical facts

Important decision rule

Focus on the user’s main intent, not the presence of historical words.

If the historical reference is incidental, label NOT_HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING.

Output format

Return JSON:

{
  "label": "HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING" | "NOT_HISTORY_FACT_SEEKING",
  "confidence": 0.0–1.0,
  "rationale": "<brief explanation>"
}

Keep the rationale under 25 words.
